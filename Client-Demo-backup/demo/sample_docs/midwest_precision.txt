Midwest Precision Components — Operations Handbook
Revision 7.2, Effective January 2026

SECTION 1: FACILITY OVERVIEW

Midwest Precision Components operates a 68,000 square foot facility in
Groveport, Ohio, employing 84 people across three shifts. The plant produces
precision-machined components for the agricultural equipment and hydraulic
valve industries. Annual revenue for the most recent fiscal year was 14.2
million dollars.

The facility operates three CNC machining cells, one dedicated turning cell,
and a finishing department with vibratory deburring and passivation lines.
Primary customers include two Tier 1 agricultural OEMs and one hydraulic
distributor. No single customer represents more than 32 percent of revenue.

SECTION 2: QUALITY MANAGEMENT

The quality management system is certified to ISO 9001:2015, with the most
recent surveillance audit completed in November 2025. Zero major
nonconformities were issued. Two minor nonconformities were recorded: one
concerning calibration record retention, one concerning supplier
re-evaluation timing. Both were closed within the required 30-day window.

First-pass yield across all machining cells averaged 97.3 percent in the
most recent quarter. The target is 98.0 percent. The largest single source
of scrap is tool breakage on Cell 2, accounting for 41 percent of
scrapped parts. A tool-life monitoring pilot is scheduled for the second
quarter.

Customer complaints totaled 7 in the last twelve months, down from 14 in
the prior period. All complaints were resolved within 15 business days.
No product recalls have occurred in the company's 22-year history.

SECTION 3: MAINTENANCE AND RELIABILITY

Preventive maintenance is scheduled through a CMMS and executed by two
full-time maintenance technicians. Overall Equipment Effectiveness across
the plant averaged 74 percent last quarter. The CNC cells averaged 79
percent; the finishing department lagged at 63 percent, primarily due to
unplanned downtime on the passivation line.

Mean time between failures on the passivation line was 142 hours last
quarter, against a target of 250 hours. The primary failure mode is pump
seal degradation, attributed to inadequate filtration of the recirculating
solution. A filtration upgrade was quoted at 18,000 dollars with an
estimated 14-month payback.

Spare parts inventory is valued at 62,000 dollars. Critical spares are
identified in the CMMS. The maintenance manager reviews spare parts
consumption monthly and adjusts reorder points quarterly.

SECTION 4: SUPPLY CHAIN

The plant maintains 47 active suppliers. Of these, 12 are designated
strategic suppliers representing 78 percent of purchased material spend.
Raw material lead times averaged 4.2 weeks last quarter, down from 6.8
weeks during the prior-year period.

Steel bar stock is the largest single commodity purchased, at approximately
1.1 million dollars annually. The plant holds 3 weeks of bar stock on
hand as buffer against mill lead-time variability. Aluminum and brass are
held at 2 weeks.

Supplier on-time delivery averaged 94.1 percent last quarter. The target
is 96 percent. Two suppliers are on formal corrective action plans for
repeated late deliveries.

SECTION 5: SAFETY

The plant recorded zero lost-time injuries in the most recent calendar
year. Three recordable incidents occurred: two minor lacerations requiring
first aid, and one strain injury from manual lifting. The strain injury
prompted a review of lifting aids in the shipping department.

The safety committee meets on the first Wednesday of each month at 6:30 AM.
Membership includes the plant manager, the safety coordinator, two hourly
representatives from each shift, and the maintenance manager. Meeting
minutes are posted on the shop floor bulletin board within 48 hours.

Lockout/tagout procedures are reviewed annually. The most recent review
identified 14 energy isolation points that required updated labeling. All
were corrected within 60 days.

SECTION 6: CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT

The plant runs a formal kaizen program with a target of 24 events per year.
In the most recent year, 27 events were completed. Documented savings
totaled 412,000 dollars, against a program cost of 96,000 dollars.

The largest single kaizen saving came from a setup reduction project on
Cell 3, which reduced changeover time from 38 minutes to 9 minutes and
generated estimated annual savings of 118,000 dollars.

A second significant project addressed material handling between the
machining cells and finishing, reducing work-in-process inventory by 34
percent and freeing 1,200 square feet of floor space for a new deburring
station.

SECTION 7: WORKFORCE

Total headcount is 84, comprising 61 hourly production employees, 12
salaried support staff, 6 engineers, 3 maintenance technicians, and 2
senior managers. Average tenure is 8.4 years. Voluntary turnover last
year was 11 percent, against a regional manufacturing average of 16
percent.

The plant operates an apprenticeship program in partnership with a local
community college. Four apprentices are currently enrolled. Two apprentices
graduated into full-time machinist roles in the past year.

Cross-training matrices are maintained for all production roles. The
current average is 2.7 qualified operators per position, against a target
of 3.0. The turning cell is the thinnest at 2.1 operators per position.
